3.0   NATIONAL DEVELOPMENT PLAN FRAMEWORK
Lufwanyama Town Council operates within its mandate to deliver local public goods to the people in the 
District and to discharge all or any functions set out in Article 147 (2) (c) of the Constitution of Zambia 
and Local Government amendment Act No 2 of 2019. The Council aims to improve the quality of lives for 
the people of Lufwanyama through the provision of efficient, effective, high quality, equitable and 
sustainable public services in a transparent and accountable manner with full stakeholder participation.
Lufwanyama Town Council aims to contribute significantly in the development of the District through 
the provision of efficient, effective, sustainable, public services in an environmentally friendly manner. In 
addition, the Council will strive to provide public goods by fostering community participation in 
development and  enhancing trade and commerce in the district through a robust infrastructure 
development.

Development Fund (CDF), the inclusion of Cash for Work (CFW) under the Social Protection and 
Community Development. Local Revenue accounts for 22 percent of the budget amounting toK23 
million while K82.2 million is National Support accounting for 78 percent of the total budget. The budget 
has been allocated to 15 Programmes as tabulated in table 2.

The Constituency Development programme has been allocated K36.1 million in the 2025 budget 
representing an increase of 18 percent from then K30.6 million in 2024. Costituecncy Development Fund 
(CDF) alone represents 34.2 percent of the total budget. Much of this allocation, that is, K17.7 million is 
channelled towards implementation of Community Projects identified from the Integrated
Development Plan and approved by the Constituency Development Fund Committee (CDFC). It is
essential to note that 5 percent of Community Projects is deducted as a Disaster Component- in this case
K929, 316.88. Secondary Boarding School and Skills Training Bursaries amount to K6.2 million. Youth,
Women and Community Empowerment funds are apportioned such that K2.5 million is allocated for
Grants while K3.7 million is meant for Loans. In order to smoothly operate and implement the fund, K1.8
million is the Administrative Cost- which is 5 percent of CDF. Lastly, fuel totalling K3.3 million for
Constituency Road Maintenance has been allocated.
The Local Governance program has been allocated K3.7 million in 2025 representing 3.5 percent of the
total budget. The Legislative Function subprogramme is apportioned K3.6 million for Council Meeting
and National Events while Citizen Engagement has K32, 600 to enhance activities of the Ward
Development Committees.
Integrated Development Planning comprises three sub-programmes, namely, Environmental Planning,
Spatial Planning and Socio-Economic Planning. The programme has been allocated K2.8 million
representing 2.7 percent of the total budget. Spatial Planning has received the largest share standing at
K1.6 million for purchase of a Geographical Information System (GPS), survey and development control.
Socio-economic Planning stands at K932, 060 for interventions that directly affect the standard of living
and the development of the human being himself. The launch of the Integrated Development Plan is a
vital component to mention. Lastly, Environmental Planning has been allocated K287, 249 for activities
that include planting trees and minimising the adverse effects of climate change.

Public Health and Environmental Protection programme has been allocated K1.4 million representing
1.4 percent of the total budget. This is an important programme as it is not accidentally but through
consistent implementation of prudent preventive measures that cholera has not yet heavily assaulted
Lufwanyama. Solid Waste Management has been allocated K459, 710 for maintenance of the dump site 
and for fuels for weekly disposal of garbage. Water Supply and Sanitation Services has K402, 803 for
drilling of boreholes and repair of existing ones. The Health Inspection allocation stands at K400, 289 for
improving compliance levels through routine inspection of mines and businesses. Pest Control has K117,
500 chiefly for Indoor Residual Spraying and registration, vaccination and cropping of dogs. Lastly,
Cemetery and Funeral Services has been allocated K57, 860 for burial of unclaimed bodies and cleaning
the cemetery.
The Housing and Community Amenities programme has been allocated K 10 million representing 9.6
percent of the total budget. The Roads and Drainages subprogramme has the biggest share amounting
to K4.7 million- owing to the fact that Lufwanyama is a rural district that is dire need of a revolutionary
improvement of the road network infrastructure. This is followed by Public Housing with K3.8 
millionseeing Lufwanyama has a shortage of descent housing. K860, 000 has been channelled towards 
Markets and Bus Stations- again Lufwanyama has neither a proper township market nor bus station. 
Extending the coverage of the existing Street Lighting has been allocated K681, 661 in the 2025 budget. 
Lastly, Parks and Gardens have been allocated K50, 000 for development of a park in the township.
Under the Recreation, Culture and Religion programme, the total allocation is K1.3 million for the 2025
budget. The Sports Promotion subprogramme takes up to 99 percent of the whole allocation- to be
spent mainly in talent identification throughout the district. It will also be utilised in improving sports
infrastructure in the district as much as possible. The Cultural Affairs programme stands at K12, 224 for
contributions towards the three gazetted traditional ceremonies in the district.
Education and Skills Development has been allocated K19, 241 representing 0.02 percent of the total
2025 budget. The whole amount is being channelled towards the only subprogramme, District Archives.
The Local Authority has also attached an officer from among the existing members of staff to be a key
custodian of District Archives.
Public Order and Safety Programme has been allocated K3.6 million representing 3.4 percent of the total
budget for the Local Authority. The programme comprises three subprogrammes which include
Community Policing, Fire Protection Services, and Building Regulations. Firstly, Community Policing has
been allocated K1.9 million to ensure that there is law and order and safeguarding public properties.
Secondly, Fire Protection Services has K1.7 million for responses towards fire incidences and general
public order and safety. Lastly, Building Regulations has been allocated K18, 100 for ensuring compliance
in buildings and enforcement of stop order and demolition notices.
The Management and Support Services programme has been allocated atotal of K11.4 million
representing 10.8 percent of the total budget. This programme has eight subprogrammes. Human
Resource and Administration has been allocated K4.5 million; Public Relations have K201, 398; Auditing
has K552, 609; Procurement Management has K1 million; Information and Communication Technology
has K545, 098; Executive Manage has been apportioned K1.2 million; Accounting Management stands at
K3.1 million; and Legal Services estimated at K405, 974.
Resource Mobilization and Management Programme represents 4.5 percent of the total budget with an

allocation standing at K4.8 million. There is only one subprogramme to which the whole amount has

With a total amount of K4.6 million, the District Health services programme represents 4.4 percent of
the total budget. Primary Health Services has been allocated K1.7 million for Technical Support Services,
Mentorship and Health Sector Planning. District Health Services has been allocated K432, 516 mainly for
administrative and management purposes. Hospital Services has been allocated K2.5 million for
providing comprehensive curative care, diagnostic services, maternal health including emergency
services, supportive services, maintaining and improving infrastructure and administrative services for
the smooth running of hospital activities.
The Transport Services programme has been allocated K3.2 million. This allocation will be channeled
towards the only subprogramme, Road Transport. This is very important for both goods reaching
markets and inputs getting to farmers. There is no alternative to a good transport network. The works to
be implemented include grading township and feeder roads.
Agricultural Services represents 0.5 percent of the total budget having an allocation totalling K573, 918
to be distributed to the three subprogrammes that constitutes it. Firstly, Agricultural Crop Production,
Advisory and Technical Services has been allocated K410, 000. Secondly, Agribusiness Development and 
Marketing is standing at K40, 000. Thirdly and lastly, Agriculture Coordination has been apportioned
K123, 918.
The Fisheries and Livestock programme equally represents about 0.5 percent of the total budget having
been allocated K486, 160 in 2025. The first subprogramme is Fisheries and Livestock Marketing standing
at K21, 000. Secondly, Animal Health Services with K122, 650. Thirdly, Fisheries Production and
Productivity Improvement standing at K106, 650. Fourthly, Livestock Production and Productivity
Improvement with an allocation of K80, 000. Lastly, District Fisheries and Livestock Coordination with
K155, 860.
The Social Protection and Community Development programme has been allocated K21.4 million
representing 20.3 percent of the 2025 district budget. The District Social Welfare subprogramme is
allocated over 99 percent of the allocation since the Community Development subprogramme has only
K5, 172. The funds will be utilised chiefly in supporting the livelihoods of vulnerable and marginalised
people of all categories in Lufwanyama.

The Fisheries and Livestock programme is exceedingly essential in Lufwanyama- a district that is quickly 
transitioning into an agricultural and livestock hub on the Copperbelt Province. One of the Key Outputs is 
training farmers in enterpreneurship, value addition, trade facilitation and market linkages. All livestock 
farnmers in the district are to be adequately provided with Veterinary [extension] Services. This will include 
generation of five (5) Animal Disease Surveilance reports between January and December of 2025. 
Fisheries Production and Productivity is aimed at improving extension services by targeting to reach out to 
all fish farmers in Lufwanyama. It is the aim of the department to promote the production of quality 
fingerlings, the use of low-cost feeds, create linkages to input suppliers as well as create market linkages. 
Sustainable Fish Development is a key output with regards to fish in natural water bodies- and eight (8) fish 
ban patrols are planned to be conducted in 2025.
Participation at Agricultural Shows, and continued engagement with the local community alongside the 
provision of technical support and purposeful administration are factors that are meant to remarkably 
improve Fisheries and Livestock in the district throught 2025.
