3.0   NATIONAL DEVELOPMENT PLAN FRAMEWORK
To provide efficient and affordable services equitably, community engagement and observance of good 
financial management and accountability. This is in agreement with the Republican Constitution 
(Amendment) Act No.2 of 2016 Part IX on the System of Devolved Governance [Article 147 (2)] and Part 
XI on the System of Local Government.
Lufwanyama Town Council will strive to contribute to the the development of the district through the 
provision of efficient, effective and sustainable public services. To achieve this, the Council will take 
advantage of it's comparative advantage through engagement with the mining companies housed in the 
district and also exploiting the immense fertile agriculture land. In addition, the Council will strive to 
provide public goods by fostering community participation in development and  enhancing trade and 
commerce in the district through a robust infrastructure development.

4.0   BUDGET SUMMARY
The 2026 Estimates of Revenue and Expenditure for Lufwanyama Town Council is projected at 
K147.3million, representing a 39.7% increase from the 2025 budget of K105.4million. This growth is 
primarily driven by anticipated national support, including the upward adjustment of the Constituency 
Development Fund from K36.06 million to K40.00 million, an increase in the Devolution Support Grant 
from K2.7 million to K39.4 million and the provision of the Land Development Fund amounting to K8.9 
million. Additionally, the Council expects a rise in its own-source revenue from K23million to K27million. 
This total budget has been proportionally allocated to 15 programs as indicated in table 1 under budget 
allocation by programmes.

Constituency Development Programme has been increased to K40 million. The Community Projects Sub 
Programme receives K24.2 million (60.5%) to fund local infrastructure projects such as road 
maintenance, community centers, and water points. The Women and Youth Empowerment Sub 
Programme receives K7 million (17.5%) to support skills development, entrepreneurship, and 
empowerment initiatives. The Secondary School and Skills Development Bursaries Sub-Programme also 
receives K7 million (17.5%) to provide bursaries for secondary school students and vocational skills 
training. CDF Administration is allocated K1.8 million (4.6%) to ensure efficient programme management 
and monitoring. 
Local Governance Programme has been allocated K3.3million. The Legislative Function Sub-Programme 
receives K3 million to fund Council Committee meetings, legislative activities, and policy development. 
The Local Elections Sub-Programme receives K319, 400 to cover costs related to electoral preparations 
and conducting local elections. Citizen Engagement is allocated K33, 600 to promote community 
participation in local governance. 
Integrated Development Planning Programme totals K6.6million. Spatial Planning Sub Programme 
receives K5 million to support urban and land use planning, zoning, and infrastructure alignment, while 
Socio-Economic Planning Sub-Programme receives K1million to fund local economic development 
programmes, social development planning, and policy research, while environmental planning accounts 
for K543,803. 
Public Health and Environmental Protection Programme totals K2.7 million. Solid Waste Management 
receives K755,020 for collection, transport, and disposal of municipal waste. Cemetery and Funeral 
Services receives K201,524 for cemetery maintenance and burial services. Health Inspections receives 
K495,839 to monitor food safety, hygiene standards, and sanitation. Water Supply and Sanitation 
Services receives K1 million to improve water supply and sanition in the district. Pest Control is allocated 
K271,724  to manage mosquito breeding areas, stray animals, and public health risks. 
Housing and Community Amenities Programme is allocated K51.7million. Roads and Drainages Sub 
Programme receives K9.2million for road rehabilitation, drainage maintenance, and flood prevention 
works. Markets and Bus Stations receives K28,170 for formation of market committees. Public Housing 
receives K42.1million to maintain and upgrade public housing estates.

In 2026, the Constituency Development Programme focuses on strengthening community development, 
improving service delivery, and enhancing community participation. The Council will ensure that one 
hundred percent (100%) of all approved community projects are fully implemented; demonstrating a strong 
commitment to delivering planned interventions. To improve access and mobility, the programme includes 
the maintenance of thirty (30) kilometers of roads, ensuring safe and reliable connectivity across 
communities. Disaster response remains a priority, with the Council aiming to implement one hundred 
percent (100%) of projects under the disaster category against those reported.. Transparency and visibility 
will be promoted through the branding of one hundred percent (100%) of completed CDF assets. 
The programme will ensure that one hundred percent (100%) of eligible secondary school bursary applicants 
and one hundred percent (100%) of eligible Skills Development Youth bursary applicants are supported, 
promoting equitable access to educational and vocational opportunities for young people. In addition, one 
hundred percent (100%) of Women & Youth Group grant applicants and one hundred percent (100%) of 
Women & Youth Group loan applicants will benefit from the empowerment programmes, ensuring 
inclusivity and equitable access to financial support. Overall, the 2026 targets reaffirm the Council’s 
commitment to delivering impactful community interventions, maintaining critical infrastructure, 
strengthening oversight structures, promoting transparency, and expanding educational and economic 
opportunities under the Constituency Development

In 2026, the District will prioritise the improvement of health outcomes through strengthened maternal and 
child health services, control of communicable diseases, enhanced HIV/AIDS interventions, and improved 
health system performance. These interventions will be coordinated under the leadership of the 
Lufwanyama District Health Office, with a strong emphasis on preventive, promotive, and curative health 
services. To reduce maternal mortality, the District will scale up access to skilled maternal health services by 
increasing the availability and utilisation of trained health personnel. In this regard, the District has set a 
target for 75 percent of all deliveries to be conducted by skilled personnel in 2026. Antenatal care services 
will also be strengthened, with a target of increasing the proportion of pregnant women receiving at least 
eight antenatal care visits. Child health services will focus on strengthening the Expanded Programme on 
Immunisation (EPI) to ensure full immunisation coverage, while nutrition interventions will aim to reduce 
the proportion of children with severe malnutrition (below -3 Z-score) from 3 percent to 2 percent.
The District will intensify efforts to prevent and control communicable diseases. Surveillance and case 
management will be strengthened to reduce the incidence of hepatitis, typhoid, and syphilis, with targeted 
reductions in new cases recorded across all three conditions. Disease surveillance systems will be enhanced 
through the continued monitoring of Acute Flaccid Paralysis (AFP) cases and the holding of four (4) epidemic 
preparedness committee meetings to ensure readiness for public health emergencies. Malaria remains a 
major public health concern in the District. Building on previous progress, the District aims to further reduce 
malaria incidence from 1,367 per 1,000 population in 2023, to 1,112 per 1,000 population in 2024, and 
ultimately to 850 per 1,000 population in 2026. This will be achieved through sustained distribution and 
utilisation of insecticide-treated nets, increased indoor residual spraying of households, and strengthened 
case detection and treatment at health facility and community levels.
To address the burden of tuberculosis, the District will ensure early detection, treatment, and follow-up of 
all TB cases. All presumptive TB cases identified will be investigated, with a target of 100 percent treatment 
and cure rates for confirmed cases. This approach is expected to significantly reduce TB transmission and 
improve patient outcomes. In line with national and global targets, the District will continue implementing 
the 95-95-95 strategy. By 2026, the District targets 95 percent of people to know their HIV status, 95 
percent of those diagnosed to be linked to care, and 95 percent of people living with HIV to achieve viral 
suppression. Additional HIV prevention and care interventions will include voluntary medical male 
circumcision for 3,500 males and cervical cancer screening for 827 women, contributing to reduced HIV 
transmission and improved reproductive health outcomes. 
To enhance service delivery and accountability, the District Health Office will continue providing technical 
support and supervision to lower-level health facilities. Performance assessments will be conducted in all 36 
health facilities, with an increase in the proportion of technical support visits conducted from 52 percent to 
60 percent. Quality improvement and quality assurance initiatives will also be implemented to strengthen 
clinical standards and patient safety. Hospital services will be strengthened to ensure continuity of care and 
improved patient welfare. The District plans to provide food rations to approximately 7,000 patients, 
procure medicines and medical supplies in all four quarters of the year, increase the number of surgical 
operations conducted in theatre to 90, and continue providing emergency response through ambulance 
services, ensuring timely referrals and lifesaving interventions.
